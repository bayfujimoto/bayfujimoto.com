# Site Concept

## Overview
This website is a personal archive staged through objects, documents, records, and browse systems. It is not a conventional portfolio. It should feel like a finding aid to a life, presented through material evidence rather than abstract personal-brand sections.

The central premise is that what a person keeps, uses, makes, consumes, and accumulates often reveals more than a polished biography. The website should therefore describe a life as a constellation of traces: documents, logs, scans, notes, artifacts, and residues.

## Experience goal
The user should feel that they are not merely navigating a website, but handling a collection.

The site should feel:
- archival
- intimate
- textured
- orderly
- careful
- specific
- restrained

The site should not feel:
- generic
- corporate
- overly theatrical
- horror-themed
- game-like
- cluttered
- precious

## Conceptual references
Primary references:
- archival hierarchy and finding-aid logic
- desks, binders, folders, ledgers, trays, drawers, packets
- item inspection logic similar to Resident Evil, but without horror tone

The Resident Evil influence is specifically about focused object attention:
- an item is isolated
- enlarged
- examined carefully
- turned, flipped, zoomed, or unfolded
- accompanied by descriptive text and contextual information

This project should borrow that intensity of inspection without inheriting horror aesthetics.

## Structural idea
The homepage is a desk with seven objects: six that navigate the archive, one that remains unresolved.

The six navigable objects are:
- **Guide** — key on a paper tag (meta-object; finding aid)
- **Identity** — dossier (expansion into three documents)
- **Labor** — powder-coated steel strongbox (contraption)
- **Consumption** — interlocking composite sphere (expansion into five pieces)
- **Creation** — stone stamp and paper (contraption)
- **Accumulation** — string-tied bundle (contraption)

The **seventh object** — an amber block — sits at a slight remove and its purpose remains unresolved.

Objects divide into two interaction types:
- **Expansions**: clicking causes the object to separate into component parts, each independently navigable (Identity, Consumption)
- **Contraptions**: clicking initiates a sequence of state changes that must be completed before the series opens (Guide, Labor, Creation, Accumulation)

Opening an object reveals a category interior.
The category interior reveals subcollections.
Subcollections reveal record systems.
Individual records open into an inspection view.

## Design implications
The site should preserve the visual reality of materials:
- folds
- margins
- dates
- wear
- annotations
- paper edges
- labels
- storage logic

But material metaphor must never replace orientation. The archive must remain understandable through headings, structure, metadata, filtering, and browse systems.

## Content logic
The site is made of four layers:
- objects: emotional and navigational entry points
- documents: scans, pages, ledgers, folders, sheets, ephemera
- text: labels, metadata, captions, contextual notes
- systems: tabs, indexes, timelines, calendars, filters, cross-links, lists

Not everything needs a fake physical shell. The stronger object language belongs at the top and bottom of the experience:
- top: homepage desk objects
- bottom: item inspection

The middle layers should emphasize clarity.

## Long-term goal
The final site should be able to grow over time without losing coherence. It should support ongoing additions, not just a one-time launch. It should act as a living archive with stable structures for logs, artifacts, projects, and ephemera.
