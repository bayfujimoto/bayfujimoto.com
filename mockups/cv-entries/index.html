CV — inspection card studies

Six real records (CV-2026-001 … 006). The CV entries have no reproduction, so two questions are open at once: the structure (one card whose strip holds every entry, or one card per entry behind a browse grid) and the plate (what a record with nothing to scan puts in the square). They are independent — any plate fits either structure.

rule Everything is built on the live card and grid (fields left, plate right; ruled rows; strip under the plate; ↑/↓ and ←/→ as on the site). New treatments are variations within those structures, marked MOCKUP in shared.css.

reference

00Today — the document box The CV as it renders now: one bordered document, entries grouped by category, scrolling. Kept for comparison.

structure A — one card, shared strip

01Timeline plate, span glyphs in the strip The CV is one record, as the Guide is. The plate keeps the calibrated plate's grammar with a year scale in place of millimetres; the entries are bars in two columns (education, employment) and the selected one is lit. Each strip frame is that entry's span on a tiny rail. ↑/↓ step. 03Specimen plate, typographic tiles — and scans where they exist Same structure; the plate sets the entry as a printed label (organization in serif, role and dates in mono, the category as a stamp). Where an entry has a scanned document, the real thing takes the plate at true scale — the B.A. frame shows one.

structure B — one card per entry

02Browse grid + a standard card per entry The CV becomes a subcollection like films or books: a year-grouped grid of typographic tiles (item-grid--cv), each opening the standard card; ←/→ step between entries, no strip. Shown with the specimen plate; the timeline plate would work here too, highlighting the open entry.
