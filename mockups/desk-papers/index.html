Desk papers — layout test

The five series as bundles of documents — two to four sheets clipped together — in place of the five containers (dossier, box, sphere, stamp-and-paper, bundle). Three things stay dimensional: the key, an amber block, and the stamp, which lies beside Creation's sheet as its instrument rather than its entry. The test asks two questions: does a pile of paper still read as a scene, and can every entry be seen and hit when the papers overlap.

what is tested Flat CSS, no WebGL. Lit from the top of the screen as the live spot is (shadows fall down-screen). Rev 2 follows the reference collage closely: every paper lies inside one open folder (a manila folder over a blue one, offset), the sheets are square to the folder (±0.5°), small pieces overhang its edges, and there are no file labels — each document names itself. The five bundles are the folder's columns: a narrow left column, two tall centre sheets, a portrait blue drawing, a right column. On the phone the same composition is fitted to the width (legibility deferred). The hit areas toggle outlines every button's footprint; objects hides the key, amber and stamp.

Rev 3 is the material pass: grain on every sheet and on the folder; ragged and torn edges; tape; fold creases and corner wear on the drawing; coffee rings; a rubber stamp and a seal with ink dropout; handwriting (Homemade Apple for the cursive, Caveat for the notes) and pencil sketches with a displacement filter. The Accumulation column and the film ticket are real scans from the archive — the display derivatives of EPH-2026-006, 010, 013, 014, 017, 018 and 029, copied into assets/ so the mockup opens offline. That is the argument in one column: the archive already holds the material the reference is made of.
